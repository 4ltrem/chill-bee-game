## Claims Verified

### Claim 1: Many sweat bees have brilliant metallic green colouring
**Source:** World Wildlife Fund Canada, "World Bee Day: Native Pollinators"  
**URL:** https://wwf.ca/stories/world-bee-day-native-pollinators/  
**Quote:** "Take the bicoloured sweat bee (*Agapostemon virescens*), which is metallic green towards its front end and boldly striped on its back half."  
**Source kind:** NGO  
**Date checked:** 2026-11-01  
**Note:** The claim is narrowed to the extent that certain sweat bee species (like *Agapostemon virescens*) display metallic green colouring; the source does not claim all sweat bees are metallic green.

### Claim 2: Approximately 170 sweat bee species in Canada, all nesting underground
**Source:** Government of Canada, "COSEWIC Assessment and Status Report: Sable Island Sweat Bee (2014)"  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/sable-island-sweat-bee-2014.html  
**Quote (species count):** "Approximately 170 described species of sweat bee occur in Canada."  
**Quote (nesting):** "Sweat bees are a diverse, widely distributed group of bees" that are "predominately ground-nesting."  
**Source kind:** Government  
**Date checked:** 2026-11-01  
**Note:** Source says "predominately" ground-nesting; not all species nest underground, but most do.

### Claim 4: Leafcutter bees construct nests by cutting leaf material and arranging it in dead wood
**Source:** Pollinator Partnership Canada, "Bees"  
**URL:** https://pollinatorpartnership.ca/en/bees  
**Quote:** "leaf-cutter bees (*Megachile* spp.), which prefer dead trees or branches for their nest sites"  
**Source kind:** NGO  
**Date checked:** 2026-11-01  
**Note:** Source confirms nesting in dead wood and the preference for that habitat; the journal's claim about cutting leaves and lining/capping nests is plausible based on common leafcutter bee behaviour (see Claim 7 on pollen-carrying morphology), though this specific source does not explicitly describe the cutting and lining behaviour.
